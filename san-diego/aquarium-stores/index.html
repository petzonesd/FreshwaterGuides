Aquarium and Fish Stores in San Diego

By Pet Zone SD Team · Updated September 22, 2026 · 11 min read

For a city its size, San Diego has an unusually deep local fish-store scene: reef and coral specialists clustered around Kearny Mesa and Miramar, a couple of long-running general pet stores with serious aquatics departments, and a freshwater/aquascaping side that's smaller but active. This guide is a straightforward rundown of who's actually open right now and what each one is good for, so you can pick the right stop instead of driving across town to the wrong one.

One disclosure up front: this site is published by Pet Zone Tropical Fish, a San Diego retailer, and Pet Zone is one of the stores listed below. We're not pretending otherwise, and we're not going to tell you it's the only good option — it isn't. Everything else on this page is a store we have no financial relationship with, included because it's genuinely useful for a local hobbyist to know about.

Other San Diego-area stores

These have no connection to Pet Zone. Addresses and hours below were checked against each store's own site or a recently updated listing as of September 2026 — small independent shops do change hours, so it's worth a quick call before a special trip.

Aquatic Warehouse

5466 Complex St, Suite 204, San Diego, CA 92123 (Kearny Mesa). Phone (858) 467-9297. Hours: Monday–Saturday 10:00am–6:00pm, Sunday 11:00am–6:00pm. The emphasis here is advanced reef and custom fish systems, but the store also supports planted freshwater tanks and pond keeping, so it's a reasonable one-stop if your household runs more than one type of tank.

Kung Fu Corals

6506 El Cajon Blvd, Suite D, San Diego, CA 92115 (Mid-City/City Heights). A coral-focused shop built around a WYSIWYG model — the piece you see photographed is the exact piece you get, not a same-species substitute. Strong pick for LPS, SPS, and soft coral specifically, with a smaller general fish selection. Walk-in retail hours are limited: Tuesday, Wednesday, Friday, and Saturday, 12:00pm–6:00pm.

Aqua SD

7550 Miramar Rd, Suite 130, San Diego, CA 92126 (Miramar). Primarily a coral and marine-livestock shipper with a walk-in showroom — daily 10:00am–6:00pm, but they ask for a call at least 45 minutes ahead for local pickup rather than just showing up. Good option if you want to see coral in person before it ships, or if you're a reef keeper who wants direct access to a livestock supplier rather than a general aquarium store.

Aquatica Corals and Custom Aquariums

500 Rancho Santa Fe Rd, Suite 101B, San Marcos, CA 92078. This one's in North County, roughly 25–30 minutes from downtown San Diego depending on traffic, so it's really for hobbyists in the Escondido/San Marcos/Vista corridor rather than a casual city drive. Phone (760) 597-0445. Hours: Tuesday–Friday 12:00pm–7:00pm, Saturday–Sunday 11:00am–6:00pm, closed Monday. Reef and coral focus, plus custom aquarium installation and maintenance services — worth knowing about if you're planning a built-in tank rather than a standard glass box.

Pet Kingdom

3191 Sports Arena Blvd, San Diego, CA 92110 (Midway District, near the Sports Arena). Phone (619) 224-2841. Hours: Monday–Saturday 10:00am–6:00pm, Sunday 10:00am–5:00pm. Open since 1982 and billed as the largest independent pet store in San Diego — it's a general pet store first, but the aquatics department is substantial and covers both freshwater and saltwater, plus a reptile section if that's also on your list. Useful if you want to compare a broad big-store-style selection against the smaller specialty shops.

Buying online and having fish shipped

If you're outside reasonable driving distance — North County beyond San Marcos, out of the county entirely, or you just don't have a car-friendly afternoon — several of the stores above, particularly the coral specialists, ship livestock nationally with overnight or expedited delivery built around minimizing transit stress. It's a legitimate way to stock a tank when there's nothing comparable nearby, and coral in particular tends to travel better than most fish.

That said, don't treat shipping as equivalent to a store visit by default. A fish or coral piece that's spent 12–24 hours in a bag inside a box has had a harder day than one that walked out the door with you, and you lose the chance to actually look at it — clear eyes, even fins, steady swimming — before you commit. If a local option exists for what you're after, it's usually the safer first choice; shipping is the fallback, not the default.

Whichever store you buy from, don't skip your tank's nitrogen cycle to get fish in faster. A tank that isn't cycled yet is the single most common reason new fish die within the first week, regardless of how healthy they looked in the store.

Local clubs

Beyond the stores, San Diego has two active hobbyist clubs worth knowing about if you want opinions, trades, and swap meets rather than just retail:

  • San Diego Tropical Fish Society (sdtfs.org) — a general freshwater club. Public meetings are held the second Sunday of each month at 6:00pm in Room 101, Casa del Prado, Balboa Park. Guests are welcome, not just members.
  • Koi Club of San Diego (koiclubofsandiego.org) — focused on ponds and koi specifically. They run an annual show at the Del Mar Fairgrounds, which is a good way to see serious koi up close before deciding whether a pond is a realistic project for your yard.

What to check before you buy at any store

Regardless of which shop you walk into, a few signs are worth checking before you put a fish in your bag, because a store's overall care standard tends to show up in its tanks:

  • Water clarity. Cloudy or visibly dirty water in display tanks usually means inconsistent maintenance, which raises the odds of stressed or sick stock.
  • Fish behavior. Healthy fish hold their fins up and swim with purpose. Clamped fins, gasping at the surface, or fish hanging listlessly in a corner are worth asking about directly — a good shop will tell you if a tank's been having issues rather than hide it.
  • Mortality tanks kept separate. Reputable stores quarantine new arrivals and pull visibly sick fish out of display tanks rather than leaving them in with healthy stock.
  • Staff who ask questions back. A shop that asks about your tank size and current stock before selling you something is generally more trustworthy than one that'll sell you anything you point at — this matters most for stocking decisions, which is its own can of worms covered in our compatibility guide.

If you're newer to the hobby and not sure what to stock first, it's worth reading up before you shop rather than at the counter — our beginner freshwater fish guide covers species that tolerate a new keeper's mistakes, and if you're leaning toward a planted setup specifically, your first planted tank walks through what to ask a specialty store like Pet Zone Convoy or Aquatic Warehouse for.

What's the single best fish store in San Diego?

There isn't one — San Diego's stores split by specialty (reef and coral, freshwater and aquascaping, monster fish and koi, general pet-department aquatics), so the right one depends on what you're stocking, not a universal ranking.

Do any of these stores require an appointment?

Kung Fu Corals keeps limited walk-in hours (Tuesday, Wednesday, Friday and Saturday, 12–6pm). Aqua SD asks for a 45-minute heads-up call for local pickup since it runs primarily as a shipping operation with a showroom. The rest keep regular daily retail hours — call first anyway, since small shops change hours without much notice.

Are there local fish clubs in San Diego?

Yes. The San Diego Tropical Fish Society (sdtfs.org) covers general freshwater keeping and meets the second Sunday of each month at Casa del Prado in Balboa Park; the Koi Club of San Diego (koiclubofsandiego.org) covers ponds and koi and runs an annual show at the Del Mar Fairgrounds. Both welcome guests.

I don't live near San Diego — can I still buy from these stores?

Several of them ship livestock and dry goods nationally, coral specialists especially. It's a reasonable option if there's nothing comparable near you, but a fish that's driven or walked out the door tends to arrive in better shape than one that's spent a day in a box — buy locally first if you have the choice.
